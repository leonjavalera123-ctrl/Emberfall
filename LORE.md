# EMBERFALL: 1940
### World Bible & Faction Lore — v1

*A real-time strategy game in the spirit of Command & Conquer: Red Alert.*
*An alternative Earth. An alternative 1940. Steam, iron, and stolen starfire.*

> **The pitch:** In 1861 the sky fell and seeded the world with emberstone — a mineral
> that made steam engines mighty and lightning tameable. The old nations starved, broke,
> and reformed into four new powers. Now, in 1940, the largest untouched emberstone field
> ever charted has been found beneath a dead kingdom — and all four powers have sent
> their armies. You are a **Commander**. Gather emberstone, raise your base, fortify it,
> and erase the enemy from the map.

---

## PART I — HOW THIS WORLD WENT WRONG

This is not our Earth. Its history bent in 1861 and never bent back.

- **1861 — The Emberfall.** For nine nights the sky burned. Meteor storms fell across
  every continent, and buried in the fallen rock was **emberstone** — a dull grey ore,
  cold and dead until heated, and then... not.
- **1862–1869 — The Long Winter.** The dust of the Fall veiled the sun. Seven years of
  grey skies and black harvests. The old empires — proud, ancient, certain — starved to
  death in their palaces. The old map died with them.
- **1870s — The Ember Rush.** The survivors discovered what emberstone does: a fistful,
  once ignited, burns furnace-hot for weeks. Steam engines that once filled a mill house
  now fit on a wagon, then on a man's back. Whoever held ember veins held the future.
  The Rush redrew every border in blood and claim-stakes.
- **1877 — The Roil.** Emberstone seeded the seabeds too. The oceans turned violent —
  endless magnetic storms, compasses spinning, iron hulls dragged down. Deep water
  became the wall of the world. Sea trade died; the sky inherited it.
- **1893 — The Palewater Disaster.** Scholars learned emberstone holds *charge* as well
  as heat. The first arc-capacitor lit the city of Palewater for six glorious minutes —
  then burned it to glass in a single night. Arc research was outlawed across the
  civilized world as "the Devil's spark."
- **1901 — The Covenant Schism.** The banned arc-scholars refused to stop. Led by
  Doctor Cassian Vael, they stole the Palewater shard and vanished into the auroral
  north. The world forgot them. This was a mistake.
- **1914–1922 — The First Ember War.** The new powers finally collided over the ember
  veins. Eight years of trench lines, land ironclads, and cinder shells — fought almost
  entirely across the lowland garden-kingdom of **Veyre**, which had the misfortune of
  lying between everyone. The war ended in exhaustion, not victory. Veyre was left a
  poisoned grey waste the survivors call **the Ashfall**.
- **1938 — The Cradle.** Deep-bore prospectors under dead Veyre struck the impossible:
  a virgin emberstone field larger than every known vein combined. They named it the
  Cradle. The news could not be contained.
- **1940 — The Cradle War.** Four powers. One motherlode. No treaties left worth the
  paper. *This is where the game begins.*

## PART II — EMBERSTONE

The resource. The reason. The thing your harvesters will be gathering in-game.

| Form | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Burned** | A fist-sized stone fires a boiler for weeks. Powers everything: tanks, trains, forges, cities. |
| **Charged** | Holds electrical charge like a battery. The foundation of arc weaponry — coils, capacitors, shields. Outlawed everywhere except where it isn't. |
| **Dust** | Poison. Ember dust ruins lungs, kills soil, and never washes out. The Ashfall is proof. |

**In-game:** emberstone deposits are the map's primary resource. Harvesters mine it and
haul it to your Refinery. Every faction burns it. One faction refuses to — and charges
it instead.

---

## PART III — THE FOUR POWERS

Every faction fields soldiers, tanks, aircraft, support vehicles, and base defenses —
but each is *shaped* by what it survived. Specialization is doctrine, not accident.

---

### 1. THE KARVATH IRON CONCORD — "The Anvil of the World"

**Colors:** rust-red and black iron | **Symbol:** a hammer over a mountain
**Specialty:** heavy armor, artillery, fortification | **Playstyle:** the slow hammer

Before the Fall, the Karvath Massif was a poor country of miners and toolmakers —
mocked by the lowland kingdoms as "the people who live in holes." Then the Long Winter
came, and the holes were the only warm places left in the world. The mountains held the
deepest ember veins anywhere, and ember-fired furnaces kept the mining towns alive while
the proud kingdoms below froze in their marble halls. Refugees climbed the passes by the
hundred thousand and paid their passage in labor.

Out of that migration the Concord was forged: the mine-barons, the engineers' guilds,
and the furnace-priests fused into something that is part state, part company, and part
church of iron. Karvath children still memorize the *Ledger of the Winter* — the tally
of every soul the old kingdoms let starve — and the lesson the Concord took from it is
carved over every foundry gate: **"Iron remembers."** Never again dependent. Never again
soft. Everything the Concord builds, it builds twice as thick as it needs to be.

Karvath does not rush. Karvath *arrives*. Its land dreadnoughts move at a walking pace
behind curtain walls of rolling steel, and its doctrine — *the Wall That Walks* — treats
war as an engineering problem: establish the line, advance the line, and let the enemy
break themselves against it. The Concord's warmaking voice is **Forge-Marshal Odann
Kray**, a mine-collapse survivor with a pneumatic arm who speaks in short sentences and
has never once retreated. Not out of pride, officers say. He simply builds no roads
behind him.

**Arsenal (steam-heavy, riveted, absurdly overbuilt):**
- **Infantry:** *Boilerplate Grenadiers* (steam-armored heavy infantry, slow and tough), *Forge Guard* (engineer-soldiers who repair vehicles and walls in the field)
- **Tanks:** *Bastion* heavy steam tank (twin guns, the Concord workhorse), *Hammerfall* siege mortar crawler (outranges all static defenses)
- **Aircraft:** *Kondor* armored gunship (slow, heavily plated, cannon-armed — Karvath's grudging admission that the sky exists)
- **Support:** *Mule* steam hauler (harvester), *Ironroad* mobile rail-layer (extends supply lines)
- **Superunit:** ***Juggernaut* land dreadnought** — a rolling fortress with a crew of thirty and a naval gun. The ground shakes first. Then you see it.
- **Defenses:** granite-and-steel curtain walls (strongest barriers in the game), *Anvil* steam-hammer turret, mortar pits

**Strong:** armor, siege range, walls, attrition. **Weak:** speed, air cover, cost per unit.

### 2. THE AURELIAN LEAGUE — "The Thousand Sails"

**Colors:** azure and brass | **Symbol:** a gull over a rising sun
**Specialty:** air power, speed, reconnaissance | **Playstyle:** hit-and-run, own the sky

The Aurel Archipelago was the merchant crossroads of the old world — a league of island
principalities grown fat and brilliant on sea trade. The Emberfall spared their cities,
and for one gilded decade the Aurelians believed they had escaped history. Then the Roil
came, and the sea — their mother, their bank, their entire reason to exist — turned
against them. Compasses spun. Iron hulls went down with all hands. Within five years,
the greatest navy ever assembled was rusting at anchor.

The League's answer is remembered in a single sentence from Sky-Regent Adora Vantelle's
address to the Admiralty in 1879: **"The sea has betrayed us, so we shall marry the
sky."** The merchant houses poured their fortunes into mooring towers, ember-lift
airships, and gyro-yards. Cities climbed their own mountains to build sky-harbors above
the cloud line. Aurelian territory today is not measured in land — it is measured in
*routes*: trade lanes, wind corridors, and refueling aeries stitched across the whole
map of the world. Half its air fleet is privateers flying under letters of marque,
which the League insists is a perfectly respectable arrangement.

Aurelia fights the way it trades: fast, mobile, and allergic to fair exchanges. Its
doctrine — *Who Holds the Sky Holds the Map* — trades positions instead of blows: scout
everything, strike the weak seam, and be gone before the answer arrives. The current
**Sky-Regent, Lyra Vantelle**, great-granddaughter of Adora, was elected at thirty-one
after personally leading the relief convoy through the Roil's edge to the starving
sky-city of Vell — a feat every rival house called impossible, which is why she did it.

**Arsenal (brass, canvas, propellers, elegance):**
- **Infantry:** *Sky Marines* (glider-dropped shock troops — deploy anywhere), *Cloudwatch Corps* (spotters with telescopes and radio; reveal the map)
- **Vehicles:** *Dart* armored car (very fast, very thin), *Zephyr* flak half-track (mobile anti-air)
- **Aircraft:** *Sparrowhawk* biplane fighter (best dogfighter in the world), *Wasp* gyrocopter (hover gunship), *Pelican* air transport (carries troops and light vehicles)
- **Superunit:** ***Stratos Leviathan* carrier zeppelin** — a flying airfield launching Sparrowhawk sorties. The League's cities-in-miniature, and its pride.
- **Defenses:** flak batteries, searchlight towers (detect infiltrators), tethered mine-balloons

**Strong:** air supremacy, speed, vision, map control. **Weak:** fragile ground forces, expensive losses, weak static defense.

### 3. THE ASHFALL COMPACT — "The Meek Who Refused"

**Colors:** olive drab and rust | **Symbol:** a green weed growing through a cracked helmet
**Specialty:** cheap numbers, salvage, sabotage, entrenchment | **Playstyle:** the thousand cuts

Veyre was the garden of the old world — the lowland kingdom whose wheat fed three
continents. Its only crime was geography: it lay flat, fertile, and directly between
every power that wanted to strangle the others. When the First Ember War came, it was
fought in Veyre's fields, and the great powers' cinder shells salted the earth with
ember dust until the garden turned grey. Then they signed their treaty — in a city none
of them had wrecked — and went home. Nobody rebuilt Veyre. Nobody thought there was
anyone left to rebuild it.

They were wrong. The survivors came up out of the cellars and the flooded trench lines
wearing scavenged filter masks, and they got to work. The Compact is not a nation in
any way the other powers recognize — it is a web of salvage crews, tunnel families,
cellar schools, and militia cells, bound by an oath older than paperwork: *no one eats
unless everyone eats.* Their first tanks were First-War wrecks winched out of the mud
and made to run again. Their parliament is a radio frequency. The great powers called
them rats, and the Compact took the insult as a field manual: rats survive everything,
go everywhere, and cannot ever, ever be fully exterminated.

And now the Cradle — the greatest prize in history — has been found *directly beneath
their homes*. To the Ashfallers this is the final insult arriving on schedule: the
thing that murdered Veyre was under its floorboards all along, and here come the fires
again. The Compact's war leader, **Warden Mara Sixt**, ran a cellar school through the
First War before she ever ran a militia, and her standing order carries a teacher's
patience: *"Everything the great powers throw away, we throw back."* The Compact does
not win battles. It makes battles cost more than they're worth, forever.

**Arsenal (scavenged, retrofitted, dug-in, dirt cheap):**
- **Infantry:** *Conscript Militia* (the cheapest soldier in the game — and the most numerous), *Cinder Sappers* (gas-masked demolition troops), *Vulture Crews* (capture enemy vehicles and **harvest wrecks for resources** — the Compact's signature economy)
- **Tanks:** *Rat* tankette (tiny, fast, swarms), plus anything they steal from you
- **Aircraft:** *Duster* attack plane (a converted crop-duster with rockets — ugly, cheap, effective)
- **Support:** *Burrow* tunneling rig (digs tunnel entrances; infantry move underground between them)
- **Superunit:** ***The Ashworm*** — an armored war-train that lays its own track, bristling with guns. A moving fortress and a moving supply line.
- **Defenses:** trench networks (infantry cover), barbed wire, minefields, hidden cellar bunkers (concealed until they fire)

**Strong:** cost, numbers, salvage economy, stealth, dug-in defense. **Weak:** low tech ceiling, individually fragile units, no native heavy armor.

### 4. THE LUMINAR COVENANT — "The Second Sun"

**Colors:** bone-white and arc-violet | **Symbol:** a small sun rising over a horizon line
**Specialty:** arc weaponry, energy shields, elite units | **Playstyle:** few but mighty; protect the grid

When Palewater burned, the world needed someone to blame, and the arc-scholars were
convenient. Laboratories were torched, journals banned, and the study of ember-charge
was declared heresy against nature. The scholars' answer came from Doctor **Cassian
Vael**, the disaster's most brilliant survivor, who stood trial with his burns still
bandaged and said only: *"You are angry that we dropped the torch. But you would have
us put out the light."* The night before his execution, his students broke him out —
and stole the Palewater shard on the way.

The exiles marched north beyond the tree line, where the aurora meets the ice and no
sane power would follow. There, around the great shard, they raised **Solyn Vaul, the
Glass City** — the only city on Earth lit by electric light. Over forty years the
Covenant grew into something between an academy and a faith. Its creed: emberstone is
not fuel. It is *inheritance* — the frozen light of a dead star — and shoveling it into
boilers is desecration. The Covenant does not burn the stone. It **charges** it, and
with it they built what the world banned: arc lances, capacitor hearts, walls of
woven lightning.

The Covenant watched the First Ember War through telescopes, in silence, and made a
vow: the world cannot be trusted with the fire. When word of the Cradle broke, the
Glass City emptied for the first time in a generation. Understand this about the white
columns advancing behind their shield-domes: they have not come to *take* the Cradle.
They have come to take it **away from everyone**. Archlumen Vael is ancient now, his
pulse kept by a clockwork-and-copper heart, his voice arriving by arc-radio like
weather: *"We are not your enemy. We are your consequence."*

**Arsenal (copper coils, vacuum tubes, humming violet light):**
- **Infantry:** *Arc Templars* (lightning-lance elite infantry — chain damage through grouped enemies), *Mirror Wardens* (carry personal shield projectors, screen other units)
- **Vehicles:** *Faraday* spider-walker (arc turret on six legs, walks over walls), *Pylon* shield crawler (projects a protective dome over nearby units)
- **Aircraft:** *Seraph* rocket-glider interceptor (fast, dives from high altitude)
- **Support:** *Dynamo* generator wagon (mobile power — Luminar units fight harder inside the grid)
- **Superunit:** ***The Cathedral*** — a storm-zeppelin that charges the clouds themselves and calls down lightning barrages.
- **Defenses:** *Lightning Spire* (tesla-coil tower), *Aegis* dome projector (energy barrier over structures) — all of it dependent on the power grid

**Strong:** strongest individual units, shields, chain lightning vs swarms. **Weak:** tiny armies, everything is expensive, and if the power grid falls, the Covenant falls with it.

## PART IV — HOW THEY MATCH UP

Soft counters — any faction can beat any other, but the terrain tilts:

| | vs Karvath | vs Aurelia | vs Ashfall | vs Luminar |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Karvath** | — | struggles: slow columns under bombs | grinds through swarms | favored: artillery outranges shield domes |
| **Aurelia** | favored: bombs ignore walls | — | struggles: cheap flak everywhere | even: speed vs shields |
| **Ashfall** | struggles: swarms break on armor | favored: their losses cost 10x yours | — | struggles: arcs chain through swarms |
| **Luminar** | struggles: outranged, out-massed | even | favored: lightning loves a crowd | — |

**The cycle:** Aurelia beats Karvath beats Luminar beats Ashfall beats Aurelia.

## PART V — THE CRADLE WAR (the game itself)

**The battlefield:** dead Veyre — grey plains, flooded trenchworks from the First War,
ruined garden-cities, and emberstone glittering in the craters. Every skirmish map is a
piece of the Ashfall, which means the Ashfall Compact is always, technically, home.

**You are a Commander.** Each power has chartered expeditionary Commanders — you —
with one mandate: hold the Cradle. In-game that means:

1. **Gather** — deploy harvesters to emberstone fields; protect the supply line.
2. **Build** — HQ, Refinery, Barracks, Vehicle Works, Airfield, Power (Luminar
   especially), each faction with its own visual style and build quirks.
3. **Customize & fortify** — walls, gates, trenches, turrets, minefields, shield
   domes; base layout is a real decision, not decoration.
4. **Destroy** — victory when the enemy HQ (and their ability to rebuild) is gone.

**Faction economy twists (lore → mechanics):**
- **Karvath:** standard economy, but walls and defenses are cheaper and tougher — turtling is viable.
- **Aurelia:** can air-drop units and buildings-in-crates anywhere scouted — logistics from the sky.
- **Ashfall:** Vulture Crews harvest destroyed vehicles (yours *and* theirs) for resources — every battle feeds them.
- **Luminar:** units and defenses are power-linked — dominant inside the grid, dying outside it.

**Superweapons (late game, one per faction):**
- Karvath — *Worldhammer*: a continental siege gun. Slow to load. Ends map squares.
- Aurelia — *Tempest Protocol*: a called-in armada bombing run across a targeted lane.
- Ashfall — *The Undermine*: a tunnel packed with explosives, detonated under any enemy structure.
- Luminar — *Second Sun*: an artificial arc-storm — lightning saturation over a zone.
